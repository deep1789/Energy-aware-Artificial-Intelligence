\begin{abstract}
Federated learning on wearable and IoT devices spends a scarce resource twice: battery energy when the model is trained, and again every time it is used. We study federated training of multi-exit networks, where a device can train only the first blocks to save energy, and ask how clients, depth, upload precision and local epochs should be chosen under per-device energy budgets. A convergence bound shows that depth heterogeneity creates an error floor that grows with the share of selected clients that skip a block, and gives the coverage needed for a target accuracy. We turn this into EcoFed, a drift-plus-penalty controller with per-device energy queues, a shadow energy price and block-coverage queues, which costs $O(N|\mathcal C|)$ per round. We evaluate it on three public benchmarks (UCI HAR, PAMAP2, Speech Commands; person-wise splits, 16 paired seeds, equal 14-trial tuning budgets for all eight methods) with an assumed, not measured, energy model. On label-skewed PAMAP2 at a binding budget EcoFed raises the final accuracy by 15 to 28 points over seven baselines and the early-stopped accuracy by 9 to 17 points over the energy-blind ones (Holm-corrected). On near-IID UCI HAR its early-stopped accuracy is within 1.1 points of all baselines while it uses up to 2.0 times less fleet energy, 1.7 to 4.2 times less energy to a target accuracy than FedAvg-type methods, and, at the two looser budgets, drains 0.4--9\% instead of 16--31\% of the devices for most baselines (at the tightest budget it drains more, 14\%, because the baselines stop early). On Speech Commands it is worse than several baselines, and a static capability-based depth rule beats it at the tightest budget on PAMAP2 when training can be stopped at the best checkpoint. Ablations show that the energy queue and depth control are essential, that the coverage queue protects the deep exits, and the advantage grows when radio energy is expensive. Confidence-gated exits cut inference energy by 1.3 to 7.2 times, and serving overtakes training energy within 12 minutes to 1.7 days of fleet operation.
\end{abstract}
