The shadow price $\lambda$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:score} moves EcoFed along an energy--accuracy frontier. Baselines have no comparable knob: their operating point is the one at which they stop. Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto} shows the validation-selected checkpoint (accuracy against the fleet energy spent up to it) for four values of $\lambda$ with all other settings fixed at their tuned values, on the first eight evaluation seeds; the sweep is reported in full and was not used to select anything. At $\phi=1$ and $\lambda=0.1$ EcoFed reaches 88.3\% on UCI HAR with 147~J, the same accuracy as FedAvg (88.3\% with 272~J) and within 0.3 pp of the best multi-exit baselines (FedAvg-Paced 88.6\% with 117~J, Energy-greedy 88.5\% with 166~J); with $\lambda=1$ it reaches 86.5\% with 67~J, i.e.\ 75\% less energy than FedAvg for 1.8 pp. On PAMAP2 at $\phi=1$ the same family gives 57.5\% with 383~J ($\lambda=0.1$) and 52.9\% with 209~J ($\lambda=0.3$), against 51.1\% with 381~J for FedAvg, 50.6\% with 219~J for Oort-style and 57.4\% with 317~J for single-exit FedAvg. At $\phi=0.25$ on PAMAP2 EcoFed with $\lambda\in\{0.03,0.1\}$ reaches 54.2\% and 53.0\%, above every baseline's best (single-exit FedAvg: 47.8\%). On UCI HAR at $\phi=0.25$, EcoFed with $\lambda=0.1$ (88.0\%, 65~J) is on par with FedAvg-Paced (88.1\%, 62~J). In short, the lower checkpoint accuracy of the tuned EcoFed on UCI HAR in Table~\ref{tab:main} is a choice of operating point---made by the validation-AUC criterion, which rewards frugality---rather than a limit of the method; a practitioner who values accuracy over energy can move up the frontier at a cost of 30 to 90~J.
