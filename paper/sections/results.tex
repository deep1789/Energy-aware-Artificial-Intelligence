\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

All statements below refer to the evaluation seeds (16 per method and setting for the main comparison, 8 for ablations and sensitivity studies), to test subjects that never appear in training or validation, and to \emph{model-based} energy (Section~\ref{sec:energymodel}). ``Significant'' means a Holm-corrected paired $t$-test at $p<0.05$ within a (dataset, $\phi$) setting across the seven baselines; the full paired tables are in \ref{app:paired}. Differences are in percentage points (pp) of test accuracy.

\paragraph{PAMAP2 (strong label skew): large gains at binding budgets}
At $\phi=0.25$ EcoFed's final accuracy is 57.5\%, against 29.2--42.8\% for the baselines; the paired differences are $+15.0$ to $+28.3$ pp and significant against all seven baselines, including Static-Depth ($+15.0$ pp, 95\% CI $[11.6,18.3]$). At the validation-selected checkpoint it reaches 57.0\% against 40.1--47.7\% for the six energy-blind baselines ($+9.3$ to $+17.0$ pp, significant), and 55.9\% for Static-Depth ($+1.1$ pp, CI $[-3.3,5.3]$, not significant). At $\phi=0.1$ the final accuracy (42.0\%) is again significantly higher than that of every baseline ($+4.4$ to $+16.2$ pp), but with early stopping Static-Depth is clearly better: 54.0\% against 41.5\% ($-12.5$ pp, CI $[-17.9,-7.0]$, Holm $p=0.004$), whereas EcoFed's advantage over the six other baselines at the checkpoint ($+2.6$ to $+6.9$ pp) is not significant after correction. Static-Depth's final accuracy is 37.6\% and its last exit is essentially untrained (9.2\% accuracy), so it is the best choice at the tightest budget only if the operator can detect the peak and stop and does not need deep exits. At $\phi=1$ EcoFed's final accuracy (57.6\%) is tied with Static-Depth (57.7\%) and FedAvg-Paced (57.6\%) and significantly higher than that of the five others ($+5.7$ to $+11.8$ pp); at the checkpoint no difference is significant (EcoFed 54.8\%, others 52.4--59.8\%).

\paragraph{UCI HAR (near-IID labels): equal accuracy for less energy}
In UCI HAR every client sees all six activities, only the person and the sensor placement change, and all methods reach 84--89\% within a few rounds (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}, top). At the validation-selected checkpoint EcoFed is statistically indistinguishable from all baselines in all three budgets (differences between $-1.1$ and $+0.8$ pp). Its final accuracy is significantly higher than every baseline at $\phi=0.1$ ($+1.8$ to $+3.2$ pp: energy-blind methods lose accuracy once their batteries run out), higher than FedAvg-Q8, FedProx and FedAvg by 2.5--3.3 pp at $\phi=0.25$, and tied at $\phi=1$. The saving is in energy: over the run EcoFed uses 2.0 times less fleet energy than FedAvg at $\phi=1$ (187~J against 379~J) and 1.15 times less at $\phi=0.25$, and only 0.4\% of devices end below 2\% battery against 16--31\% for the baselines other than Static-Depth (4.7\%). At $\phi=0.1$ the baselines stop early because remaining batteries cannot afford a full-depth round and leave 28\% of the capacity stranded, whereas EcoFed uses 77\% of it and ends with 14\% of devices drained.

\paragraph{Speech Commands (small clients, 12 classes): EcoFed is not the best method}
Here the result is mixed to negative. At $\phi=0.1$ EcoFed's final accuracy is higher than that of every baseline ($+1.6$ to $+4.7$ pp; significant only against FedAvg) and its checkpoint accuracy is tied with all of them except FedAvg ($+5.9$ pp). At $\phi=0.25$ EcoFed is better than Oort-style, Energy-greedy and FedAvg in final accuracy ($+2.7$ to $+3.2$ pp) but worse than FedAvg-Paced ($-6.9$ pp, CI $[-8.9,-4.9]$) and, at the checkpoint, worse than FedAvg-Paced ($-7.8$ pp), FedAvg-Q8 ($-3.2$ pp) and Static-Depth ($-2.6$ pp). At $\phi=1$ it is significantly worse than all seven baselines at the checkpoint ($-2.5$ to $-6.1$ pp) and than four of them in final accuracy. EcoFed spends roughly half the energy of the others on this dataset (12 against 20~J at $\phi=0.25$, 51 against 95~J at $\phi=1$) and is frugal by configuration: the shadow price $\lambda$ selected at $\phi=0.25$ is reused at $\phi=1$, where it is too high (Section~\ref{sec:pareto}), but even with the lowest price in our sweep EcoFed does not reach the accuracy of the best baselines at $\phi=1$. We do not have a tested explanation. A plausible one is that with only 71--184 clips per client and a speaker-level feature shift, deeper local training and more epochs matter more than cheap breadth of participation.

\subsection{Energy to reach a target accuracy}
Fig.~\ref{fig:eta} reports the fleet energy needed to reach 90\% of the accuracy that FedAvg reaches when energy is not scarce. On UCI HAR EcoFed needs 6.5, 7.3 and 13.9~J at $\phi=0.1$, $0.25$ and $1$, which is 1.7 to 4.2 times less than FedAvg, FedProx, FedAvg-Q8 and FedAvg-Paced (all significant), 2.2 to 2.5 times less than Oort-style and Energy-greedy at $\phi\le0.25$, and the same as those two at $\phi=1$ (ratios 1.19 and 0.98, not significant). Static-Depth is tied with EcoFed at $\phi=0.1$ (ratio 0.96) and slower at $\phi=0.25$ and $1$ (1.5 and 1.7 times, significant). On PAMAP2 the target is not always reached within the horizon at $\phi=0.1$ (9 of 16 seeds for EcoFed, 15 of 16 for Static-Depth, 4 to 7 of 16 for the others), so ratios there use the seeds in which both reach it and are not significant; at $\phi=0.25$ EcoFed needs 28~J, 2.1 to 3.8 times less than all baselines except Static-Depth (ratio 1.04, tied), and at $\phi=1$ (60~J) it is 1.7 to 2.6 times faster than Oort-style, FedAvg-Paced, FedAvg-Q8, FedProx and FedAvg, and tied with Energy-greedy (1.19) and Static-Depth (1.15). On Speech Commands no method reaches the target at $\phi=0.1$, and at $\phi=0.25$ only FedAvg-Paced (9 of 16 seeds), Static-Depth (2) and FedAvg-Q8 (1) do, EcoFed never; at $\phi=1$ EcoFed (35~J) is tied with Oort-style, Energy-greedy and FedAvg-Q8 (ratios 1.03, 0.94, 1.07) and 1.3 to 1.5 times faster than FedAvg-Paced, FedProx, FedAvg and Static-Depth.

\emph{The energy queue is essential.} Without it clients keep running expensive configurations until their batteries are empty: 15 to 72 pp more devices end drained (all six settings, $p\le0.002$), up to 38~J more energy is spent, and final accuracy drops by 7.8 and 6.5 pp on UCI HAR ($p\le0.010$), by 8.2 ($p=0.14$) and 27.8 pp ($p<0.001$) on PAMAP2 and by 2.1 ($p=0.023$) and 8.1 pp ($p<0.001$) on Speech Commands, at $\phi=0.1$ and $0.25$ respectively. \emph{Depth control matters most, then epoch control.} Forcing full depth lowers final accuracy by 4.5 to 18.0 pp in every setting ($p\le0.042$) while using up to 97~J less energy because clients cannot afford the configuration. Forcing two epochs changes final accuracy by $+0.7$ to $-6.9$ pp and is significant in two of six settings (PAMAP2, $-6.9$ and $-3.1$ pp). \emph{Precision control matters little here.} Forcing 32-bit uploads changes final accuracy by at most 1.8 pp and is never significant; this is expected given the energy breakdown of Fig.~\ref{fig:energy}, where radio energy is a small share of the cost of a participation in our default setting. We return to this in Section~\ref{sec:sens}. \emph{The coverage queue protects the deep exits, at a price.} Without it the last exit loses 10.2 pp on UCI HAR at $\phi=0.1$ (not significant), 29.0 pp at $\phi=0.25$ ($p=0.040$), 21.5 and 22.9 pp on PAMAP2 ($p\le0.003$) and 30.2 pp on Speech Commands at $\phi=0.25$ ($p<0.001$); at $\phi=0.1$ on Speech Commands there is no effect (+0.9 pp). The best-checkpoint accuracy is not significantly changed in any setting, because shallow exits are enough to classify; and the final best-exit accuracy changes by $-0.1$ to $-2.3$ pp except on PAMAP2 at $\phi=0.1$ ($-13.2$ pp, $p=0.001$) and on Speech Commands at $\phi=0.1$, where removing the queue \emph{improves} it by 4.7 pp ($p=0.008$). The queue also costs energy: without it fleets spend 4 to 57~J less at $\phi=0.25$. So the coverage queue buys the quality of the whole multi-exit model, not of its best exit, and on the small Speech Commands clients at the tightest budget it is a net cost. This is the behaviour the theory predicts for the deep exits, and we test it directly next.

\subsection{The coverage floor is visible in the data}\label{sec:coverage-exp}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} predicts that, for a fixed horizon, the final gradient norm grows with $(1-\sigma)^2$. We test this with a controlled probe that removes every other source of variation: no energy limit, uniform client sampling, and a depth that is $L=4$ with probability $\sigma$ and $1$ otherwise, so that $\Pr[d\ge j]=\sigma$ for $j\ge2$ (6 seeds $\times$ 8 values of $\sigma$ $\times$ 2 datasets, 100 rounds). Fig.~\ref{fig:cov} shows the squared norm of the gradient of the full multi-exit objective at the final iterate. It rises with $(1-\sigma)^2$ on both datasets: the linear fit has slope $0.88$ and $R^2=0.14$ on UCI HAR (Spearman $\rho=0.30$, $p=0.037$) and slope $18.7$ and $R^2=0.24$ on PAMAP2 ($\rho=0.51$, $p=2\times10^{-4}$). The relation is noisy and the bound is not tight---we do not claim it is---but the sign, the monotone trend and the quadratic shape are what the theorem predicts, and the last-exit accuracy falls accordingly (from 90.3\% at $\sigma=1$ to 87.6\% at $\sigma=0.1$ on UCI HAR; Fig.~\ref{fig:cov}, right). Note that the practical aggregator used in the experiments is the sample-normalised average, not the Horvitz--Thompson weighting of the analysis; the probe therefore tests the prediction for the aggregator that is actually deployed.
