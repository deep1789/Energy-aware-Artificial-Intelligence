Training is only half of the energy bill. We deploy each trained model with the confidence-gated cascade of Section~\ref{sec:lifecycle-model}: a single threshold $\theta$ per model is chosen on validation data as the cheapest one whose validation accuracy is within one percentage point of the model's best single exit, and we then report test accuracy and modelled inference energy (fleet-average device mix). Table~\ref{tab:life} compares three deployments: single-exit FedAvg (trained with a last-exit loss; every window runs the full network), multi-exit FedAvg with the cascade, and EcoFed with the cascade. Four observations follow. \emph{First, the cascade cuts inference energy per window by a factor of 1.3 to 7.2.} EcoFed's cascade needs 118 and 130~$\mu$J per window on UCI HAR against 845~$\mu$J for the single-exit network (7.2 and 6.5 times less), at a cost of 0.7 and 1.2 pp of accuracy at $\phi=0.25$ and $1$; on PAMAP2 it needs 219 and 382~$\mu$J against 950~$\mu$J (4.3 and 2.5 times less) and is far more accurate than the single-exit model (58.5\% and 57.9\% against 22.1\% and 39.9\%; see the caveat on single-exit tuning in Section~\ref{sec:limits}); on Speech Commands the saving is small (331 and 349 against 454~$\mu$J, 1.4 and 1.3 times) because the 12-class task needs the deeper exits. The multi-exit FedAvg cascade saves less on the two wearable datasets (2.3 to 3.9 times) and almost nothing on Speech Commands (1.2 times), because its shallow exits are weak: only 23--70\% of windows leave at the first exit on the wearable datasets, against 70--97\% for EcoFed, and none on Speech Commands. Table~\ref{tab:exits} shows why: with exit weights $\lambda_j\propto j^2$ the first exit receives only 3.3\% of the loss weight of a full-depth client, and FedAvg's first exit reaches 51.5\% on UCI HAR against 86.1\% for EcoFed, whose depth-1 clients train it with the full loss weight. This is a consequence of our choice of exit weights (made to favour the last exit, \ref{app:extra}); with uniform weights the shallow exits of the energy-blind baselines would be better and their last exit worse, so the cascade comparison must be read with that choice in mind. \emph{Second, the extra training cost of making a model multi-exit is negligible.} EcoFed trains with \emph{less} energy than FedAvg in five of six settings; in the sixth (PAMAP2, $\phi=0.25$) it uses 27~J more (163 against 136~J) but saves 731~$\mu$J per window relative to the single-exit network, so by Eq.~\eqref{eq:breakeven} the extra training is repaid after $3.7\times10^4$ windows, about 40 minutes of operation of the 20-device fleet. \emph{Third, serving dominates the lifecycle.} In every row serving matches the training energy within 12 minutes to 1.7 days of fleet operation (last column of Table~\ref{tab:life}; Fig.~\ref{fig:life}), so over a month of operation inference would use roughly 18 to 3\,400 times the energy of training. \emph{Fourth, these numbers depend on the always-on assumption} (one window per 1.28~s per device for the inertial data and two per second for speech) and on modelled energies, and should be read as orders of magnitude.
